How to Fix a Leak Under the Sink (P-Trap and Drain)

Short answer: Dry everything, run water, and watch to find the exact drip. Leaks at the curved P-trap are usually fixed by reseating or replacing the slip-nut washers or swapping in a new plastic trap kit; leaks at the supply lines need a new braided connector.

Difficulty
Easy
Time
20 to 60 minutes
Updated
November 9, 2026

What is causing it?

Under-sink water comes from three places: the drain (P-trap slip nuts, the tailpiece, or the strainer where the drain meets the sink), the supply lines and shutoff valves, or the faucet itself running down the outside. Drain leaks only drip while water is draining. Supply leaks drip all the time, even with the faucet off.

How do you fix a leaking P-trap? Step by step

  1. Dry everything and find the drip

    Empty the cabinet, dry all pipes and the cabinet floor, and set a dry paper towel underneath. Run water with the stopper closed to fill the basin, then pull the stopper so a full rush of water goes down. Watch each joint with a flashlight and wipe with a fresh towel to see where water appears first.

  2. Tighten the slip nuts

    If a P-trap joint drips, hand tighten the slip nut, then a quarter turn more with pliers. Plastic nuts crack if forced.

  3. Reseat or replace the washers

    If tightening does not stop it, put a bucket under the trap, unscrew the nuts and pull the trap. Check the cone-shaped washers: the flat side goes against the nut and the tapered side faces the joint. Replace any that are cracked or flattened, or install a new trap kit.

  4. Fix a leaking drain strainer

    If water seeps from where the drain meets the sink basin, unscrew the large nut under the sink, lift out the strainer, clean off the old putty, press a rope of fresh plumber's putty under the flange, and reinstall.

  5. Replace a leaking supply line

    Shut off the valve, put a towel under it, unscrew both ends of the supply line, and install a new braided line hand tight plus a quarter turn with a wrench. Turn the valve on slowly and check for drips.

  6. Test for a full day

    Leave a dry paper towel under the joints overnight. A dry towel the next morning means you are done.

Safety notes

  • If a drain cleaner was used recently, wear gloves and eye protection before opening the trap.
  • Do not use plumber's putty on some stone sinks; it can stain. Use the sealant the sink maker recommends.

When should you call a pro?

  • The shutoff valve leaks from its stem or will not shut off.
  • Water is coming from inside the wall behind the sink.
  • Old metal drain pipes are corroded through or the trap arm in the wall is cracked.

Frequently asked questions

Should I use Teflon tape on slip nuts?

No. Slip joints seal with the beveled washer, not the threads. Tape can keep the nut from seating properly.

Why does my P-trap leak only when the sink drains fast?

A full basin draining at once puts the most water through the joints, which shows weak seals. That is why the full-basin test finds leaks better than a running tap.

Can a P-trap be too loose or misaligned?

Yes. If the pipes are not lined up straight, the washer seals on one side only. Loosen both nuts, line up the trap, and retighten.
