How to Regrout Tile

Short answer: Scrape out the damaged grout with a grout saw or oscillating tool to about 1/8 inch deep, vacuum and dampen the joints, press new grout in diagonally with a rubber float, wipe the haze with a damp sponge, and let it cure before caulking corners or sealing.

Difficulty
Moderate
Time
3 to 6 hours for a tub surround, plus cure time

What is causing it?

Grout cracks from movement and wears or stains over time. Cracked grout lets water behind the tile. If tiles are loose or the wall behind them is soft, regrouting will not fix the underlying water damage.

How do you regrout tile? Step by step

  1. Remove old grout

    Cut out the damaged grout. This Old House advises going slightly beyond the damaged area into healthy grout; Family Handyman suggests removing about 1/8 inch of depth. Work carefully to avoid chipping tile edges.

  2. Clean the joints

    Vacuum the dust and wipe the joints with a damp sponge. Leave them slightly damp, not wet.

  3. Mix and apply grout

    Mix powdered grout to the consistency on the bag, or use premixed. Hold the float at a 45 degree angle and press grout firmly into the joints with diagonal strokes.

  4. Wipe the haze

    After the time on the package, wipe diagonally with a barely damp sponge, rinsing often. Buff off any remaining haze with a dry cloth later.

  5. Caulk the changes of plane

    Let the grout dry overnight, then caulk the tub-to-tile joint and inside corners instead of grouting them, since those joints move.

  6. Seal

    Seal cement-based grout after it cures, following the sealer's timing.

Safety notes

  • Grout dust contains silica; wear a mask and eye protection and vacuum rather than sweep.
  • Wear gloves; wet cement grout can irritate skin.

When should you call a pro?

  • Tiles are loose, hollow-sounding, or the wall flexes.
  • The backer board behind the tile is wet or crumbling.
  • There is a leak into the room below.

Frequently asked questions

Can I grout over old grout?

Not well. New grout needs depth to bond. Remove at least the top 1/8 inch.

Sanded or unsanded grout?

Follow the grout maker's joint-width chart. Unsanded is usually for narrow joints around 1/8 inch or less; sanded for wider joints.

Why should corners be caulked instead of grouted?

Walls and tubs move slightly. Grout is rigid and cracks; flexible caulk handles the movement.
