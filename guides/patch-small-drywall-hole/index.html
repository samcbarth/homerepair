How to Patch a Small Hole in Drywall

Short answer: Fill nail holes and dents with spackling or joint compound in two or three thin coats, sanding lightly between. For holes from about 1 1/2 to 6 inches, cover the hole with a self-adhesive metal or mesh patch first, then feather three thin coats of compound over it, prime and paint.

Difficulty
Easy
Time

What is causing it?

Small holes come from nails, anchors, doorknobs and furniture. The key to an invisible patch is thin coats: compound shrinks as it dries, so one thick coat cracks and sinks. Larger holes need something to bridge the gap.

How do you patch a small drywall hole? Step by step

  1. Prep the hole

    Cut away torn paper and loose bits with a utility knife. Press a dent so nothing sticks out. For an anchor hole, pull or push in the anchor and trim it flush.

  2. Small holes: fill in thin coats

    Press compound into the hole with a small putty knife, then scrape across to leave it slightly above the surface. Let it dry fully. Apply a second thin coat a little wider. This Old House recommends two or three thin, smooth coats.

  3. Medium holes: apply a patch

    Center a self-adhesive patch over the hole and press it flat. It should overlap solid drywall on every side.

  4. Feather the compound

    With the 6 inch knife, spread a thin coat over the patch extending a couple of inches past it. Let it dry, then apply two more coats, each wider and thinner, to hide the edges.

  5. Sand lightly

    Sand with a fine sponge until smooth and flush. Run your hand over it; you should not feel a ridge. Wipe off dust.

  6. Prime and paint

    Prime the patch, then paint, feathering beyond the repair so it blends.

Safety notes

  • Homes built before 1978 may have lead paint. Do not dry-sand old paint until you know; follow EPA lead-safe practices.
  • Before cutting or drilling into a wall, think about pipes and wires behind it. Wiring often runs vertically beside outlets and switches.
  • Wear a dust mask when sanding.

When should you call a pro?

  • The hole is from a water leak; fix the leak and check for mold first.
  • The wall is plaster and the plaster is crumbling or loose from the lath.
  • The same crack or hole area keeps coming back.

Frequently asked questions

Spackle or joint compound?

Lightweight spackle is easy for small holes and dries fast. All-purpose joint compound is better for larger patches and feathering wide areas.

How long does compound need to dry?

It depends on thickness, humidity and the product; read the label. Thin coats of lightweight spackle can dry within an hour; joint compound often needs several hours to overnight.

Why does my patch show through the paint?

Unprimed compound absorbs paint differently from the wall. Prime the patch and paint a wider area.
