How to Reset a Tripped Circuit Breaker

Short answer: Turn off or unplug what was running on the dead circuit, open the panel, and find the breaker sitting in the middle or off position. Push it fully to OFF, then back to ON. If it trips again right away or keeps tripping, stop and call an electrician.

Electrical safety

Turn off the breaker for this circuit and confirm the power is off with a voltage tester before you touch any wire. Many places require a licensed electrician or a permit for wiring work. If anything you see does not match these steps, stop and call an electrician.

What is causing it?

A breaker trips to protect the wiring. The most common cause is an overload: too many heaters, hair dryers, microwaves or vacuums on one circuit. A short circuit or ground fault trips it instantly. AFCI breakers also trip on arcing from damaged cords or loose connections, and GFCI breakers trip on ground faults.

    How do you reset a circuit breaker? Step by step

    1. Unplug the likely culprit

      Turn off or unplug space heaters, hair dryers and other big loads on the dead outlets and lights. Resetting with the overload still connected just trips it again.

    2. Open the panel and look

      Stand on a dry floor. A tripped breaker often sits in a middle position rather than full OFF, so look for the one handle that is not lined up with the others. AFCI and GFCI breakers may show a colored indicator.

    3. Reset it

      Push the handle firmly all the way to OFF until it clicks, then back to ON. Electrical Safety Foundation International notes that skipping the OFF step is why many resets do not work.

    4. Check for a GFCI outlet

      If power is still out in a bathroom, kitchen, garage or outside, look for a tripped GFCI outlet and press RESET.

    5. Spread the load

      Plug high-wattage appliances into outlets on different circuits. A space heater and a hair dryer on the same bathroom circuit is a classic overload.

    Safety notes

    • Never replace a breaker or fuse with a higher-rated one; it removes the wiring's protection and is a fire hazard.
    • Do not remove the panel cover. Only the breaker handles are meant for homeowners.
    • If the panel is hot, buzzing, scorched, or smells like burning, do not touch it. Call an electrician, or 911 if there is smoke.
    • Stay away from the panel if the floor is wet.

    When should you call a pro?

    • The breaker trips immediately when reset with everything unplugged.
    • It keeps tripping without an obvious overload.
    • Lights flicker or dim, outlets are warm or discolored, or you feel a tingle from an appliance.
    • Your home has an older panel brand with known problems or fuses, or you want new circuits added.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why does my breaker trip when I run the microwave and toaster?

    Both draw a lot of current. On a shared kitchen circuit together they can exceed the breaker's rating. Use them on different circuits.

    Is it dangerous to keep resetting a breaker?

    Repeated trips mean something is wrong. ESFI advises calling an electrician if the problem continues.

    How do I test an AFCI breaker?

    Press its test button monthly; the breaker should trip. If it does not, have an electrician replace it.
