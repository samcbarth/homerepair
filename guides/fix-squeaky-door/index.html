How to Fix a Squeaky Door Hinge

Short answer: Tighten the hinge screws first, then tap out each hinge pin one at a time, clean it, and coat it with a light multipurpose oil or a dry graphite lubricant before tapping it back in. If a screw spins without tightening, fill the hole with glued wood slivers and redrive it.

Easy

What is causing it?

Squeaks come from metal rubbing on metal: a dry or rusty hinge pin, or a hinge that has loosened so the leaves rub. Loose screws in the top hinge also let the door sag, which can make it rub the frame.

How do you fix a squeaky door? Step by step

  1. Tighten the screws

    Check every screw in every hinge, on the door and the jamb. Tighten them snugly by hand.

  2. Fix any stripped holes

    If a screw spins, remove it, push a few glue-dipped toothpicks or a wood sliver into the hole, let the glue dry, trim it flush, and redrive the screw. This Old House recommends this method. A longer screw into the framing also works for the top hinge.

  3. Pull one hinge pin

    With the door closed, set a nail set under the pin's head and tap upward. Do one hinge at a time so the door stays in place.

  4. Clean and lubricate

    Wipe the pin clean and rub off rust with steel wool. Coat it lightly with multipurpose oil, or with dry graphite if you want to avoid drips and dust.

  5. Reinstall and swing the door

    Tap the pin back down, wipe off excess oil, and open and close the door several times. Repeat for the other hinges.

Safety notes

  • Keep a hand on the door when pins are out so it cannot fall.
  • Protect the floor from oil drips with a rag.

When should you call a pro?

  • The door or frame is cracked or split at the hinges.
  • The frame has shifted and gaps around the door are getting bigger, which can point to settling.

Frequently asked questions

Is WD-40 good for squeaky hinges?

A light spray can quiet a hinge for a while, but it is mostly a cleaner and water displacer. A dedicated oil or dry graphite tends to last longer.

Why does only one hinge squeak?

The top hinge carries the most load as the door pulls away from the frame, so it wears and loosens first.

Can I lubricate without removing the pin?

You can drip oil at the top of the pin and swing the door, but cleaning the pin works better.
