How to Stop a Smoke Alarm From Chirping

Short answer: A single chirp every 30 to 60 seconds usually means a low battery: replace it with the type printed on the alarm and press TEST. If it keeps chirping with a new battery, or the alarm is 10 years past its manufacture date, replace the whole alarm.

Set the ladder on firm, level ground, keep three points of contact, never stand on the top rungs, and look up for power lines before you raise it. Work with a helper when you can.

What is causing it?

Chirps are warnings, not alarms. Low battery is the most common cause. Sealed 10-year alarms chirp at end of life and cannot take a new battery. Hardwired alarms have a backup battery that also chirps when low. Dust, temperature swings and a battery drawer that is not fully closed can cause chirps too.

How do you stop a smoke alarm from chirping? Step by step

  1. Find the chirping alarm

    Stand under each alarm and wait. The chirp can echo, and interconnected alarms may all sound when one is the source.

  2. Check the date

    Twist the alarm off its base and look on the back for the manufacture date. NFPA says to replace alarms 10 years from that date, and to replace any alarm with no date.

  3. Replace the battery

    Open the battery drawer, install a fresh battery of the listed type, and close the drawer fully. On hardwired alarms, the backup battery is behind a door or on the side; the alarm can stay connected.

  4. Reset and test

    Reinstall the alarm, press and hold TEST until it sounds. Some models need the test button held for several seconds after a battery change to clear the warning.

  5. Clean it

    Gently vacuum the vents. Dust can cause false alarms and chirps.

  6. Replace if it still chirps

    If the chirp continues with a fresh battery, or it is a sealed 10-year alarm giving an end-of-life signal, replace the alarm. For hardwired alarms, turn off the breaker before unplugging the wiring connector, and use a replacement with a matching connector or follow its instructions.

Safety notes

  • Do not remove a battery to silence a chirp and leave the alarm empty. Replace it the same day.
  • Never paint over a smoke alarm.
  • If the alarm is sounding a full alarm, treat it as a real fire until you are sure it is not.
  • Hardwired alarm replacement involves household wiring; turn off the breaker first. If the wiring is not a simple plug-in connector, call an electrician.

When should you call a pro?

  • You are adding new hardwired or interconnected alarms.
  • The wiring at the ceiling box is damaged or does not match the new alarm.
  • Alarms false-alarm repeatedly with no smoke, steam or dust nearby.

Frequently asked questions

How often should I replace smoke alarm batteries?

USFA advises replacing hardwired alarm backup batteries at least once a year. Follow the manufacturer's instructions for battery-only models.

How long do smoke alarms last?

Replace them 10 years from the manufacture date printed on the alarm, per NFPA guidance.

How often should I test smoke alarms?

Monthly, using the test button.

Why does it chirp at night?

Homes cool off at night, and a weak battery's voltage drops as it gets colder, which can trigger the low-battery chirp.
